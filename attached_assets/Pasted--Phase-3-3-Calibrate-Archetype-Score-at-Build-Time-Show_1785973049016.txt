# Phase 3.3 — Calibrate Archetype, Score at Build Time, Show the Queue

**Mode:** Diagnose, then build. No execution path — nothing here can cancel an event, send a message, or write to PodPlay.
**Scope:** 4 items. Item 1 diagnoses before changing anything. Item 4 is the first operator-facing screen.

---

## Context

Phase 3.2 fixed the plumbing: sync errors are at zero, the legacy `attendances_unique_attendance` constraint was diagnosed and worked around, and the engagement filter now uses observed `last_visit_date` instead of the `churn_risk` model output.

The current funnel for decision `743053a0` ("Advanced Beginner Coach Led 60 & 60", 3/4 signups):

```
2,506 players
  → 2,503  after already-booked
  → 2,503  after level fit (no gate — admissionRating null)
  → 1,239  after engagement (lapsing 99 + dormant 1,150 excluded)
  →    24  after archetype (early/planner only)
       16  SMS-reachable
```

**The operator has decided this is the right shape.** Small and precise beats large and random — 16 well-chosen texts to fill one clinic spot is a good trade, and it protects the SMS channel in a way a 345-person blast would not.

Two consequences follow, and they're what this phase addresses.

**1. The archetype threshold may be miscalibrated rather than data-starved.** 2,321 of 2,506 players are `default`, and only 46 (1.8%) are `early` or `planner`. `computePlayerTiming` requires 3+ attendance rows with `booked_at < attended_at` inside a 90-day window. A member who plays twice a month cannot clear that bar even with perfect data. That may be a threshold problem, not a coverage problem.

**2. Ranking matters far more at 16 than it did at 345.** With a large pool, flat scoring was tolerable — any eligible player was a reasonable target. At 16, who ranks first is most of the value. Warm scores currently exist for 13 players club-wide.

## Item 1 — Is the archetype threshold miscalibrated?

**Diagnose before changing anything.** If the data genuinely isn't there, no threshold change helps and 46 is the honest ceiling.

**1a. Report the qualifying-row distribution.** Per player, how many attendance rows satisfy the archetype's current conditions (non-NULL `booked_at`, `booked_at < attended_at`, within 90 days)? Report the full distribution — p10/p25/p50/p75/p90 and the count at exactly 0, 1, 2, and 3+.

**1b. Separate the two possible causes.** For players at `default`, how many are there because:
- they have **no attendance rows at all** in the window (genuinely inactive — no fix possible), versus
- they have attendance rows but **fewer than 3 with usable `booked_at`** (data coverage), versus
- they have 1–2 qualifying rows (**threshold too strict** for their attendance frequency)

These need different responses. Report the split.

**1c. Test alternative parameters.** Recompute — **dry-run only** — under each of:
- 90-day window, minimum 2 rows
- 180-day window, minimum 3 rows
- 180-day window, minimum 2 rows
- all-time, minimum 3 rows

For each: how many players get a real archetype, and what's the resulting distribution across `late`/`mid`/`early`/`planner`/`default`?

**1d. Check stability.** A lead-time profile computed from 2 bookings is noisier than one from 10. For players who qualify under both the current and a looser setting, does their archetype **change**? Report the disagreement rate. If loosening reclassifies many players, the looser setting is buying reach at the cost of accuracy — say so plainly.

**1e. Recommend, then apply.** Based on 1a–1d, recommend parameters with the reasoning, apply them, and report the resulting `early` + `planner` population. If the diagnosis shows the data genuinely isn't there, **recommend no change** and say the ceiling is real.

**1f. Reconsider `default` as a hard exclusion.** Phase 3.2 changed NULL archetypes (which passed) into `default` (which is excluded). But "we don't know this player's booking pattern" is different from "this player books late." With an eligible pool of 46, excluding unknowns is a strong implicit choice.

Report what the funnel looks like if `default` is treated as a **ranking penalty** rather than a hard gate — eligible but scored below known `early`/`planner` players. Do not implement; report the counts and recommend.

## Item 2 — Warm score at candidate-build time

Phase 3.2 correctly identified that `computeWarmScore` is program-type-sensitive, so a nightly batch writing a single player-level score to `players.warm_score` would be wrong for promotion. This also matches runbook open item 6.

**2a. Compute at build time, against the target event's program type.** In the promotion candidate builder, call `computeWarmScore(playerId, clubId, programType)` for each candidate, using the program type of the specific clinic under evaluation.

**2b. Scope it to the surviving set.** Compute **after** the engagement and archetype gates, not before — that's ~24 players rather than ~1,400. Report the measured build time before and after.

**2c. Do not write to `players.warm_score`.** The score is event-class-specific. Store it on the `promotion_candidates` row for that decision, alongside the existing `score` and `reasons`. Note in `reasons` which program type it was computed against.

**2d. Verify the ranking is no longer flat.** Re-run the funnel for decision `743053a0` and report the top candidates with their warm scores. If scores are still uniformly zero, report that and diagnose why — a warm score of 0 for every candidate means the inputs aren't there either, and that's a finding rather than a bug to paper over.

**2e. Report the ranking spread** — min, max, and distribution of final scores across the eligible set. The question is whether rank 1 is meaningfully different from rank 10.

## Item 3 — Buddy signal on a small pool

Phase 3.1 reported buddy signal firing zero times. With 178 buddy pairs and a 24-person eligible pool, that may be correct — or the join may be wrong.

**3a. Verify the mechanism** on a session that *does* have buddy overlap. Find any upcoming event where a current signup has a buddy pair with a non-booked active player, and confirm the signal fires and appears in `reasons`.

**3b. If it can't fire on the current pool**, report why — small pool, few pairs, or a broken join. Distinguish these; a broken join looks identical to a genuinely empty result.

**3c. Report the expected hit rate.** Across upcoming clinics, how often would a buddy signal be available at all? If it's rare, it's a bonus signal rather than a core ranking input, and the scoring weight should reflect that.

## Item 4 — The operator queue screen

The first operator-facing surface. Replaces the Programs placeholder or extends it.

**4a. Queue view** — all open `PROMOTE` decisions, one row each:
- clinic name, start time, hours until start
- current signups / capacity
- eligible candidate count, SMS-reachable count
- decision timestamp

**4b. Detail view** — click a decision, see:

**The funnel, in full.** Every stage with survivors and the number removed. This is the most important element on the screen. A list of 16 names tells the operator nothing; `2,506 → 16` with every step visible tells them whether to trust it.

**The ranked candidate list** — name, score, warm score, and the `reasons` payload rendered legibly (level source, slot fit, buddy signal, days since last contact, engagement state).

**The excluded, with reasons** — collapsed by default, expandable. Group by exclusion reason with counts. The operator should be able to see that 1,150 players were held back as dormant and that this was deliberate.

**4c. Plain and legible.** This is an instrument panel, not a dashboard. A clear table beats charts. The operator needs to answer one question: *would I actually send this?*

**4d. No send button.** Per constraint 4. The screen exists to make the list reviewable, not actionable.

**4e. Read-only.** No writes from the dashboard — follow the existing convention that all writes go through `/api/*` endpoints, and there are no writes needed here.
